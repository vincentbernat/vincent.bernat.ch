--- title: "Fragments of an adolescent web" description: | Between 1996 and 1998, the web was in its infancy, AltaVista reigned supreme, and France lagged behind under the burden of expensive access and inadequate regulation. uuid: cee51538-9a1a-430f-9e4d-b307b9fb2407 tags: - retro-wguide --- I have unearthed a few [old articles][tag] typed during my adolescence, between 1996 and 1998. Unremarkable at the time, these pages now compose, three decades later, the chronicle of a vanished era.[^french] [^french]: Most articles linked here are not translated from French to English. The word "blog" does not exist yet. Wikipedia remains to come. Google has not been born. AltaVista reigns over searches, while [already struggling to embrace][altavista] the nascent immensity of the web[^index]. To meet someone, you had to agree in advance and prepare your route on paper maps. 🗺️ [^index]: I recently noticed that Google no longer fully indexes my blog. For example, it is no longer possible to find the [article on lanĉo][lanco]. I assume this is a consequence of the explosion of AI-generated content or a change in priorities for Google. The web is taking off. The CSS specification has just emerged, HTML tables still serve for page layout. [Cookies][] and [advertising banners][pub] are making their appearance. Pages are adorned with [music and videos][multimedia], forcing browsers to arm themselves with [plugins][]. Netscape Navigator sits on 86% of the territory, but Windows 95 now bundles Internet Explorer to [quickly catch up][microsoft]. Facing this offensive, Netscape [opensource its browser][netscape]. France [falls behind][delays]. Outside universities, Internet access remains [expensive][ft] and laborious. [Minitel][] still reigns, offering phone directory, train tickets, remote shopping. This was not yet possible with the Internet: [buying a CD online][cd] was a pipe dream. Encryption suffers from [inappropriate regulation][crypto]: the DES algorithm is capped at 40 bits and [cracked in a few seconds][des]. These pages bear the trace of the web's adolescence. Thirty years have passed. The same battles continue: data selling, advertising, monopolies.
